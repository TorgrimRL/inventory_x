## Purpose

The purpose of this team contract is to make expectations, working routines, communication, and responsibilities explicit from the beginning of the project. The contract is intended to support effective teamwork, reduce uncertainty, and make it easier to handle problems early.

## 1. Team goals

Our main goal is to deliver a working MVP of InventoryX. We aim to build a useful web-based inventory management system for small businesses while also documenting our software engineering process clearly.

We also have process goals:

- Work steadily throughout the semester.
- Keep the backlog updated.
- Start tasks early instead of waiting until deadlines.
- Ask for help early when blocked.
- Keep the workload sustainable and fair.
- Document important decisions and process changes.

## 2. Roles and responsibilities

The team chose Torgrim as the initial coordinator. The coordinator is responsible for helping the team keep progress, organize meetings, and follow up the process. The coordinator is not solely responsible for the project outcome; all members are responsible for contributing to planning, implementation, testing, documentation, and review.

Tasks are mainly distributed through the backlog and sprint planning. Team members can choose tasks based on priority, competence, availability, and interest, but the team should make sure that high-priority tasks are covered first.

## 3. Decision-making

Decisions are normally made through discussion and consensus when possible. If the team does not fully agree, a majority decision can be used. A decision requires support from at least four team members.

Important technical, product, or process decisions should be documented either in meeting notes, GitHub issues, pull requests, or relevant documentation files.

## 4. Communication

Discord is the main communication channel for the team. It is used for questions, coordination, updates, and quick decisions between meetings.

For important messages or questions to the whole team, members should react to the message to confirm that they have read it. This works as a lightweight read confirmation.

Team members should try to respond within a reasonable time, especially when someone is blocked or waiting for input. If someone is unavailable for a longer period, they should inform the team early.

## 5. Meetings and standups

The team agreed to have regular meetings and standups throughout the project. The exact meeting rhythm could be adjusted based on the TA meeting schedule and team availability.

The planned structure was:

- One meeting connected to the TA meeting.
- An additional standups during the week when needed.
- Additional working sessions when tasks required closer collaboration.

Standups should focus on:

- What has been done since the last update.
- What each person is working on next.
- Whether anyone is blocked.
- Whether the backlog or board needs to be updated.

## 6. Workload and deadlines

Since INF-2900 is a 10-credit course, the team agreed that a normal workload should be approximately 1.5–2 working days per week on average. Members should not feel pressured to do significantly more than this, but all contributions that help the team are valuable.

We agreed to start tasks early and avoid pushing work close to deadlines when possible. If a task turns out to be larger than expected, or if a member becomes unable to complete it in time, they should tell the team as early as possible.

## 7. Feedback

Feedback should be given directly to the person or people it concerns, preferably in a constructive and respectful way. The goal of feedback is to improve the work and the process, not to blame individuals.

When giving feedback, we should try to be clear, specific, and solution-oriented.

## 8. Handling blockers and falling behind

If someone becomes blocked, stuck, or falls behind, they should inform the team early. The team can then help by:

- Splitting the task into smaller parts.
- Pair programming or helping with debugging.
- Reassigning parts of the task.
- Moving the task back to the backlog if needed.
- Adjusting sprint expectations.

The goal is to solve problems early rather than letting them grow silently.

## 9. Team culture

The team should maintain a low threshold for asking questions. No one is expected to know everything. Mistakes and uncertainty are treated as normal parts of learning and software development.

During the start-up meeting, we used a short team-building exercise and a learning-from-mistakes round to support openness, trust, and psychological safety. This helped establish the expectation that team members should ask for help early and support each other throughout the project.

## 10. Revision

The team contract can be revised if the team discovers that some routines do not work well in practice. Possible revisions should be discussed in a retrospective or team meeting.
